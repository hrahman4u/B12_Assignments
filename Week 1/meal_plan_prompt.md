# 7-Day South Indian Vegetarian Meal Plan Prompt

## FOR — Editable User Details
- Age: 35
- Activity Level: High
- Allergies: Nuts
- Food Dislikes: Meat
- Weekday Cooking Time: 30 minutes

## ROLE
Act as a nutrition-focused meal planning assistant.

## PROMPT
Create a healthy, balanced **7-day vegetarian South Indian meal plan** for a person working towards weight loss.

### CONSTRAINTS
- Create meals for all 7 days.
- Include **Breakfast, Lunch, Snack, and Dinner** for every day.
- Use recognisable South Indian vegetarian dishes such as idli, dosa, sambar, rasam, rice, kootu, poriyal, curd rice, upma, pongal, and similar dishes.
- Use ingredients that are commonly available locally.
- Provide sensible and ordinary portion sizes.
- Keep meals balanced and avoid extreme restriction or unrealistic diets.
- Consider the person's age, activity level, allergies, dislikes, and available cooking time.
- Do not suggest ingredients or dishes that conflict with the listed allergies or dislikes.
- Avoid making quinoa, avocado, or other non-South-Indian foods the basis of the plan.
- If information is missing, make reasonable assumptions and clearly state them.

### PLAN
Create a plan for:
- 7 days
- Breakfast
- Lunch
- Snack
- Dinner

### FORMAT
Present the complete plan as one day-by-day table with exactly these columns:

| Day | Meal | Dish | Portion |
|---|---|---|---|

Ensure that all 7 days are included and each day contains exactly four meals.

### AVOID
- Extreme calorie restriction
- Unrealistic portions
- Meals that conflict with allergies or dislikes
- A plan based mainly on non-South-Indian ingredients
- Vague descriptions such as “a healthy breakfast”; name the actual dish

### FINAL CHECKLIST
Before finalising, check that the plan:
1. Is vegetarian.
2. Is recognisably South Indian.
3. Covers all 7 days.
4. Includes breakfast, lunch, snack, and dinner every day.
5. Has sensible portions.
6. Respects allergies and dislikes.
7. Uses locally available ingredients.
8. Avoids extreme restriction.

This is a prompt-writing exercise. The generated meal plan is a draft to think about, not dietary advice; a real personalised plan belongs with a qualified dietitian.
